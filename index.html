Do green space investments drive evictions? Evidence from Houston and Chicago

Cities (peer review)--resubmit & under review

This research investigates evidence of the effect of green space investments on eviction risk, using dynamic difference-in-differences (DiD) models. I conducted advacned causal inferential statistics and engineered automated workflows for sensitivity analysis and 1,000 iterated permutation-based placebo tests in R. Findings show that green space investments can impact eviction risks in underserved neighborhoods, under some circumstances, highlighting the need for equity-driven stratigies for urban greenning initiatives.

How do green space investments shape the rent gap? Evidence from Houston

Urban Studies (peer review)--ready for submission

This research examines how green rent gaps—divergence between capitalized ground rent under existing use and rising expectations of potential rent associated with green space—form and vary by project scale, existing park availability, and locational context with respect to Tax Increment Reinvestment Zone (TIRZ) and light rails. Using doubly robust difference-in-differences (DiD) models, we found a clearer pattern of announcement and post-opening effects for green space projects that are large in scale or in areas with low existing park space and proximate to TIRZ and light rail stations.
